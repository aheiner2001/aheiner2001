### Mindset & learning (new — from your brainstorming)

- **Imposter syndrome and staying productive**
  - A lot of imposter feelings show up as pressure to *look* busy vs. actually move forward.
  - Productivity isn't grinding 12 hours — it's doing the next honest thing when you're unsure.
  - You're new to the industry *and* shipping real tools people use — both can be true.
  - Prompt: *What does a "productive" day look like when you still feel behind?*

- **The initial shock of entering the developer world**
  - Classes teach concepts; jobs and clients teach constraints, politics, compliance, and "good enough."
  - First real projects (Sunny, Wiggy, internship) vs. homework — different stakes, different pace.
  - Feeling behind everyone on Twitter/LinkedIn vs. what local managers actually need.
  - Prompt: *What surprised you most in the first year of building for real people?*

- **AI advice feels like financial advice online**
  - You hear: "use AI for X, automate Y, 10x your workflow" — sounds great, then… how?
  - Same vibe as personal finance content: "invest early, compound interest" — true, but not actionable on day one.
  - The gap isn't *knowing* AI exists — it's building habits and knowing when to reach for which tool.
  - Prompt: *One specific workflow where advice finally became something you actually do.*

- **When AI tools finally "click"**
  - Early stage: overwhelm, FOMO, trying to use everything at once.
  - Middle: picking a few tools, using them badly, learning limits.
  - Later: it clicks — you know what to ask, what to verify, what not to outsource.
  - End state isn't "AI does my job" — it's "I have more tools available and I know which to grab."
  - You're still in the learning phase — that's worth writing about honestly.
  - Prompt: *What clicked for you recently that didn't make sense six months ago?*

- **What I'm still trying to learn** (ongoing series idea)
  - Short recurring posts: one thing per month you're actively working on.
  - Examples: system design, accessibility depth, Webflow/CMS patterns, when to stop refactoring.
  - Shows growth without pretending you've figured it all out.

### Imposter syndrome + productivity

- Imposter syndrome isn't only "I don't belong" — sometimes it's "everyone else seems ahead so I need to cram."
- Staying productive for me means: talk to the person with the problem, ship something small, get feedback, repeat.
- Building for Sunny / Wiggy helped — managers don't care if you feel like a "real" developer; they care if Tuesday is easier.
- Writing idea: **"Productivity when you don't feel qualified yet"** — practical habits, not hustle culture.

### Entering the dev world

- Initial shock: how much of the job is communication, judgment, and restraint — not just code.
- Internship + client projects hit different than labs: compliance language you can't guess at, staging before go-live.
- You're graduating soon but already have production software — imposter syndrome ignores that evidence.
- Writing idea: **"What nobody told me about 'real' software"** — short list of surprises.

### AI: the financial-advice problem

> "I hear all this stuff online and I'm like, oh that's awesome — but how do I implement it?"

- Finance podcasts: great principles, vague execution until you open an account and set up one automatic transfer.
- AI Twitter/YouTube: same pattern — demos look magic, your repo still has a weird bug at 11pm.
- Implementation beats inspiration: one tool, one use case, one week — then add the next.
- Writing idea: **"AI advice is like finance advice — here's how I actually started using it"**

### AI: slow click, then tools everywhere

- Phase 1: hear about everything, feel behind.
- Phase 2: dabble — chat for errors, autocomplete, maybe agent features.
- Phase 3: something clicks — you know *when* to use AI vs. when to read docs / ask a human / think on paper.
- Phase 4: not expert — but the toolbox feels real: scaffold, explain, refactor, draft copy, explore APIs.
- Still learning: prompting for compliance work, not over-trusting output, knowing what to learn manually.
- Writing idea: **"It didn't click overnight — how I'm learning AI as a new developer"**
